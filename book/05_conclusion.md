
# To Conclude

What began as a question of how to connect three natural landmarks in Wellington Park revealed a much more complex challenge: finding a route that balances recreational access with environmental protection.

By combining GIS-based suitability modelling with terrain-dependent walking costs, *Welly on the Way* identified and compared potential routes connecting Slump Pond, Fools Tarn and Hutchinsons Falls. Although WLC and TOPSIS used the same environmental and topographic criteria, they produced different suitability patterns and walking-track alignments. This demonstrates how modelling decisions can influence where new infrastructure might be located (Malczewski, 2006).

Importantly, neither route was without limitations. Both crossed environmentally sensitive vegetation and made limited use of existing walking tracks, highlighting the difference between a mathematically optimal route and one that is practical or environmentally responsible. Further sensitivity testing, field assessments and consideration of existing infrastructure would help refine these proposed alignments.

Ultimately, this project demonstrates the value of GIS as a tool for exploring complex environmental decisions, rather than providing a single definitive solution.

**The best path is not necessarily the shortest or easiest, but one that allows people to experience a landscape while protecting what makes it worth exploring.**
